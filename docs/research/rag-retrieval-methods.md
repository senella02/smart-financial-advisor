# RAG retrieval methods for financial research

Research report · 20 November 2026

This report compares keyword, embedding, and hybrid search, and examines where the index could live. The research question is which method finds the evidence needed for a financial answer when all methods search the same eligible documents.

The research used hourly ingestion as a working assumption. On 21 November 2026, the owner approved separate [news](../functional-requirements.md#fr-03-daily-brief-content-and-publication) and [filing](../functional-requirements.md#fr-12-financial-data-and-news) schedules.

## The problem RAG solves

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) gives the model external evidence at question time. In finance, the retriever needs to find the right passage **within the right reporting scope**. A relevant-looking paragraph can describe a different company, a different fiscal period, or a filing revised after the requested cutoff. Hourly ingestion makes new evidence available. Query-time filters determine whether that evidence belongs in a particular answer. [Lewis et al., RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401), [FR-19 grounded answers and citations](../functional-requirements.md#fr-19-grounded-answers-and-citations)

The previous capstone’s Q19 asked for FY2024 DuPont analysis of **The Siam Commercial Bank**. The answer analyzed **SCB X**, its holding company. The restructuring makes this a recurring ambiguity because the listed SCB X kept the SET ticker `SCB`. Even within the bank’s own FY2024 report, there are separate bank-only and bank-consolidated statements. A ticker identifies a listing at a point in time. A legal entity ID identifies who the filing reports on. The reporting basis identifies which numbers belong together. [Capstone error analysis, Pattern 4](../../../llmfa-capstone/docs/chie-correctness-error-analysis.md#pattern-4--wrong-entity--scope-1-case), [SET factsheet](https://www.set.or.th/en/market/product/stock/quote/SCB/factsheet), [SCB X restructuring announcement](https://www.scbx.com/en/news/scbx-share-swap/), [Siam Commercial Bank annual reports](https://www.scb.co.th/en/shareholders/financial-information)

## Keyword, embedding, and hybrid search

- **Keyword search** ranks passages by matching words or phrases. It is a strong baseline for filing codes, defined terms, and section names such as “credit risk.” It can miss paraphrases and terms expressed in a different language. Thai lexical search needs word segmentation on both the document and query sides. BEIR found BM25 a robust baseline across its datasets. PostgreSQL’s built-in `ts_rank_cd` is a different lexical ranking algorithm, so an evaluation should name which one it uses. [BEIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663), [Unicode word segmentation](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/), [PostgreSQL text search ranking](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/textsearch-controls.html#TEXTSEARCH-RANKING)
- **Embedding search** ranks passages by how close their vector representations are to the query. It may find an English passage about “geographic revenue concentration” for a Thai question about dependence on overseas sales. Its quality depends on the model and the corpus. Semantic similarity can also rank a plausible passage highly even when it lacks the particular fact or table the answer needs. [MIRACL multilingual retrieval benchmark](https://aclanthology.org/2023.tacl-1.63/)
- **Hybrid search** combines the keyword and embedding rankings. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) combines positions in the two lists without requiring a shared score scale. A weighted combination of normalized scores is another option. Cormack et al. reported gains from RRF in their experiments, while Bruch et al. found that a weighted score combination performed better in theirs. Hybrid search adds embedding and retrieval work, so its value depends on whether the two methods find complementary evidence on this corpus. [Cormack et al., RRF](https://cormack.uwaterloo.ca/cormack/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf), [Bruch et al., hybrid fusion comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934)

## Evidence constraints under hourly ingestion

### Source versions and timing

An hourly batch can bring in a new filing, a correction to an old period, or a repeat of an existing record. The index needs a stable source identifier, such as an EDGAR accession, and a way to link related revisions. Each version carries a verified reporting entity, document type, fiscal dates, source publication time, language, and original URL. Each passage carries its version ID and a page, section, or table locator. Filing structure matters because an extracted number can lose its meaning when separated from its row label, column period, currency, or unit. [EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [Financial report chunking study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05131)

Source publication time determines whether a filing was public by a historical cutoff. Ingestion time records when it reached the system. Search visibility records when the complete version became retrievable. These times can differ by an ingestion interval plus processing time. When a filing is amended, a citation to the specific source version stays interpretable after a newer version becomes searchable.

### Evidence scope

Entity and market narrow the eligible source set. Exact form, fiscal dates, and reporting basis narrow it further. For historical questions, an FY2024 filing can have a 2025 publication date. The request “latest annual report as of 31 December 2024” therefore has both a fiscal period and a publication cutoff. For news, the publication date is usually the relevant filter. [FR-01 supported stock identity](../functional-requirements.md#fr-01-supported-stock-identity), [Contracts and integration boundaries](../contracts-and-boundaries.md#financial-and-publication-meaning)

## Index capabilities and tradeoffs

- **Pinecone managed vector search.** The capstone indexed report passages and queried them with Gemini embeddings. Pinecone supports metadata filters on similarity queries. This allows a server to restrict eligible filing versions before ranking. Index writes are eventually consistent, so an acknowledged write and a searchable revision can have different timestamps. As of 20 November 2026, Pinecone lists a free Starter plan with usage limits. The cost for our corpus depends on its size, query traffic, updates, and embedding costs. The capstone demonstrates integration feasibility. Its results do not establish a retrieval advantage over PostgreSQL on this project's Thai and English questions. [Capstone retrieval tool](../../../llmfa-capstone/llm/src/tools/vector_search.py), [Metadata filters](https://docs.pinecone.io/guides/search/filter-by-metadata), [Index freshness](https://docs.pinecone.io/guides/data/check-data-freshness), [Pinecone pricing](https://www.pinecone.io/pricing/)
- **PostgreSQL alongside the source catalog.** PostgreSQL can hold document metadata, keyword fields, and embeddings together. A GIN index supports full-text retrieval. Exact `pgvector` search over eligible rows provides a reference for nearest-neighbor recall. Retrieval shares database resources, and broad exact scans may cost more than narrow issuer-specific scans. PostgreSQL is the project’s accepted database for application state. [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-supabase-postgres-for-shared-and-private-data.md), [Supabase hybrid search](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/ai/hybrid-search)
- **A dedicated search engine such as OpenSearch.** Keyword and vector retrieval can share a search index with built-in rank or score fusion. The search index needs to stay synchronized with document versions, issuer IDs, and reporting periods in the source catalog. [OpenSearch hybrid search](https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/vector-search/ai-search/hybrid-search/index/)
- **A vector-focused engine such as Qdrant.** Dense and sparse retrieval with metadata filtering can be compared in one engine. Its Thai lexical behavior and filtered recall need evaluation. A separate index carries the same document synchronization problem. [Qdrant hybrid queries](https://qdrant.tech/documentation/search/hybrid-queries/)

Index placement and ranking method are separate questions. A narrow issuer filter can interact with approximate vector search even when the SQL predicate is correct. For example, pgvector can apply the filter after scanning an approximate index, returning fewer eligible candidates. An exact filtered query offers a reference when comparing that tradeoff. [pgvector filtering](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector#filtering)

## How to compare the methods

Use a labeled set of Thai and English questions with the expected reporting entity, fiscal period, filing version, and supporting passages or tables. Include SCB versus SCB X, an ambiguous ticker, a Thai question over an English filing, an annual versus quarterly question, an amendment after a cutoff, and a question with no eligible evidence. Measure cross-language cases separately. MIRACL includes Thai and English monolingual retrieval. Thai-to-English financial retrieval requires a separate test. FinanceBench offers examples of financial questions paired with annotated evidence. [MIRACL](https://aclanthology.org/2023.tacl-1.63/), [FinanceBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)

Keep the documents, chunks, filters, and result count fixed while comparing the three ranking methods. Measure how often the required evidence appears in the first *k* results, whether all evidence needed for a multi-part answer appears together, and whether any returned passage violates scope. Check citation support separately from retrieval so a failure to find the evidence is distinguishable from a failure to use it. For index placement, compare retrieval latency and time until a new or amended report becomes searchable. The published research motivates these comparisons. Relative performance on this project’s corpus remains unmeasured.
